# Automation (car company tycoon) - Engine Design wiki page: structured summary

Source: wiki.automationgame.com "Engine Design" (29 pp, oldid=522, last modified 24 Sept 2021). Everything below is from that page; items marked NOT COVERED are absent from it. The page is a conceptual/educational guide: it gives very few hard game numbers.

## 0. Scope
- Only 4-stroke gasoline (petrol) Otto-cycle engines can be built ("As of now"). Atkinson and 2-stroke are mentioned as real-world only. Diesel is not available.
- "hundreds of possible combinations of cylinder counts, layouts, head types, internal types, fuel system types, aspiration methods, and exhaust systems".
- Otto cycle strokes: intake, compression, ignition/combustion, exhaust.

## 1. Design flow (tabs in order)
Two levels: **Engine Family Designer** (done first; defines the base), then **Engine Variant** (bottom end -> top end -> forced induction -> fuel system -> exhaust), then Summary/Testing.
Variant tab bar (from screenshots) shows: block/head icon (family), then crank/conrod/piston (bottom end), cam (top end), turbo, fuel, exhaust, with a Quality slider on several tabs.

### 1A. FAMILY (Engine Family Designer) - tabs: Engine Block | Block Material | Family Capacity | Head And Valves | Head Material (screenshot columns; Head Material is cut off, further columns may exist beyond the screenshot)
Family fixes: cylinder count and layout, materials (block, head), min/max displacement, valvetrain (head type). "if your engine family has two or more variants, you cannot modify any of the engine family's traits (cylinder count/layout, materials used, or head type)."
Advice: decide the engine's purpose first (not a 12-cyl in an economy car, not a 3-cyl in a heavy truck).

**Engine Block** (screenshot list: Inline, V 60deg, V 90deg, Boxer, with cylinder counts 3,4,5,6 shown in the pic). Text: "twelve engine block layouts and one DLC engine block layout". More cylinders = more block complexity; two-bank layouts (V/Boxer) more complex than single bank (inline).
- Inline (single head, single bank): simplest/most common; narrow, can be long; longer = more fragile (flex of block, crank, cams).
  - Inline-3 [I3]: cheapest layout, very compact, high efficiency (less internal friction). Lowest smoothness (rocking imbalance), unfavourable for performance (small displacements). Best for small budget/economy cars. Available from the beginning.
  - Inline-4 [I4]: most ubiquitous; jack of all trades; smoother and better performing than I3 but a bit more expensive/complex; lacks I3's rocking imbalance; secondary imbalance at higher RPM. Available from the beginning.
  - Inline-5 [I5]: compromise between 4 and 6; compact length, good for transverse; smoother/more powerful than I4, less pronounced smoothness difference 4->5 than 5->6. Best for larger upscale economy cars, light trucks. Available from 1970.
  - Inline-6 [I6]: among the smoothest (primary and secondary balance), larger displacement, more powerful; least efficient of inline engines (high internal friction); harder with carburettor/single point FI (distance between cyl 1 and 6); hard to fit in bay due to length; mostly longitudinal; premium/sporty RWD, medium duty trucks. Available from the beginning.
- V engines (two heads, two banks; wider than inline but usually shorter, fits more cylinders, lower centre of gravity). Two bank angles: 60deg and 90deg.
  - 60deg V: more compact than 90deg but harder to produce; favoured for transverse layouts. Available in 6, 8, 12 cyl.
    - V6 (60deg): simplest V; more space-efficient than inline 6; less smooth than I6 (different firing order), heavier, more complex; needs a balancing shaft; superior to 90deg V6 (smaller footprint, balance). Nearly all modern V6 use 60deg. FWD premium cars, minivans, crossovers, some sports. Available from 1970.
    - V8 (60deg): compromise of smoothness/power/size/cost; uncommon; less smooth than 90deg V8, needs balancing shafts (more weight, complexity, cost); narrow dimensions allow transverse install (Volvo XC90/S80, Ford Taurus SHO). Available from the beginning.
    - V12 (60deg): smoothest, most prestigious in base game; most complex and largest; smoothness from being two balanced inline-6 banks; only available at 60deg; high-end luxury/supercars. Available from the beginning.
  - 90deg V: lower centre of gravity, easier to build than 60deg, larger footprint; mostly longitudinal in larger cars/trucks. Available in 6, 8, 10 cyl (plus 16 as DLC).
    - V6 (90deg): less compact than 60deg, lower CG (shorter height; Honda NSX), less balanced/smooth, needs balancing shaft, simpler to produce, modular with 90deg V8s (e.g. GM Vortec 4300 V6). Light-duty trucks, old base models of V8 cars. Available from 1970.
    - V8 (90deg): most ubiquitous V8; superior to 60deg V8 in nearly every metric except smoothness; smoother than 60deg V8 without balancing shaft; lower CG; cheaper; can use two crank designs (cross plane / flat plane, see Crank). Classic American full-size, medium/heavy trucks, luxury/performance. Available from the start.
    - V10 (90deg): uncommon, in sports/super cars; smoother than V8 but more expensive; not as smooth as the 72deg V10 (not in game); modular e.g. Ford Triton V10 6.8 L from 5.4 V8 + 2 cyl; Viper, Huracan, R8 reference. Availability: not stated explicitly for V10 specifically (NOT COVERED).
    - V16 (90deg): most prestigious/expensive; massive power, near-perfect smoothness; DLC only (V16 Supporter Pack on Steam); real references Cadillac-16, Devel Sixteen. Available from the start (for owners).
- Boxer (two banks, two heads, but 180deg flat with one crank pin per piston - differs from a "flat 180deg V" which shares crankpins; boxer pistons move in opposite directions). Available in 4 and 6 cyl. Both available from the start.
  - Boxer-4 [H4]: simplest non-inline; best CG, short and squat, rear-mount far back for weight distribution; smoother than I4; cannot be mounted transversely (heads in way); more complex than I4 (two heads); short enough for longitudinal FWD; harder to service (spark plugs at sides); VW Beetle, Subaru Impreza/WRX, Porsche Boxster.
  - Boxer-6 [H6]: among the smoothest; like boxer 4 low and wide; more complex than I6 (two heads); can't be transversely mounted; can be longitudinal FWD; Porsche 911/Cayman, 959, Subaru, Chevrolet Corvair.

Summary of counts (derived from text): Inline 3,4,5,6; V60 6,8,12; V90 6,8,10,16(DLC); Boxer 4,6 = 13 layouts (12 + 1 DLC).

**Block Material** (screenshot: Cast Iron, Aluminium, AlSi, Magnesium; no year data given)
- Cast Iron: cheap, easy, durable, best at muffling; heavy (bad for sports cars); common on trucks/SUVs.
- Aluminium: lighter; superior emissions ratings (porous, traps pollutants); weaker/less durable, harder to work with, requires iron cylinder sleeves (which help durability); common in classic sports cars, very common now.
- Aluminium Silicate (AlSi): specialised alloy; better at bearing stresses than pure aluminium (not as good as cast iron), no iron sleeves needed so easier to produce; porous (traps pollutants); higher material cost than aluminium; common on most modern cars.
- Magnesium: lightest, best muffling; porous like Al/AlSi (reduces pollution); highest cost, hardest to work; corrodes (cannot touch coolant) so all coolant lines and cylinder sleeves must be aluminium (composite casting, expensive/complex); only for high-end sports and supercars.

**Family Capacity** (screenshot: bore - displacement cc - stroke, with +/- buttons; default 86.0 mm / 1998 cc / 86.0 mm shown; a smaller variant slider "87.5 mm / 4200 cc / 87.3 mm" appears in the bottom-end screenshot)
- Defines physical dimensions and **maximum displacement**. Bore and stroke units: mm or in. Displacement units: cc, L, or CID.
- Bore range: "The minimum and maximum bore and stroke you can use in the game are 50 mm to 120 mm."
- Formula: Displacement = pi x (bore / 2)^2 x stroke x cylinder count.
- Stroke affects displacement linearly; bore quadratically. Bore and stroke also affect external dimensions and weight: larger bore = larger/heavier block; longer stroke only slight effect on size/weight.
- Larger bore: better airflow (more valve area), more displacement. Smaller bore: smaller/lighter engine, lighter valve train (less valve float at high RPM), less pre-ignition (knock). Longer stroke: more displacement without making engine much larger/heavier. Shorter stroke: smoother and more reliable at high RPM (lower piston speed).
- Terms: square (equal; default in game 86 mm x 86 mm; e.g. Toyota 2JZ I6, 1UZ V8), oversquare (bore>stroke: high RPM/airflow, smoother, e.g. Nissan SR16VE I4 8200 RPM VVT, GM 350ci small-block V8), undersquare (stroke>bore: packaging, torque, efficiency; longer stroke raises piston speed/rod length effects; e.g. Ford Barra I6, 5.4 L modular V8, Honda K24, Nissan KA24; almost all diesel).
- Engine size menu (bottom right of designer, hidden; press upward arrow): shows L / W / H bars and fill % (screenshot: "22%", 6.0) and per-drivetrain checkmarks (FWD, RWD, AWD, 4x4). Size arrows also appear on the engine itself.

**Head & Valves (valvetrain)** - four types, "all available from the beginning of the game". Screenshot lists: Push Rod, Direct Acting OHC, Overhead Cam, Dual Overhead Cam with valve counts 2, 4, 5 selectable (3 also stated in text for SOHC). Cams: camshaft turns once per two crank rotations. Pushrod uses one cam for inline, V and boxer; DaOHC and SOHC use one cam per head (one for inline, two for V/boxer); DOHC uses two cams per head (two for inline, four for V/boxer).
- Pushrod [OHV]: cam-in-block; cheapest to manufacture; keeps heads small/low CG (good in V/boxer); only 2 valves/cyl; poor airflow; poor high-RPM (reciprocating mass, pushrod flex, valve float); no VVL but can use VVT; used in large mass-produced V8 (GM LS, Chrysler Hemi).
- Direct-Acting OHC [DaOHC]: cam in head acting directly on valves; simplest overhead cam; light, good revving but limited by poor airflow; 2 valves only; no VVL, no multivalve; slightly taller than pushrod; needs 2 cams in V/boxer; mostly phased out.
- SOHC: cam lobes act on rocker arms; valves pushed aside from centreline (better airflow); 2, 3 (two intake one exhaust) or 4 valves (two intake two exhaust); VVL compatible; more reciprocating mass than DaOHC; 2-valve SOHC costlier than DOHC/OHV; budget cars.
  - Bore-area airflow example: A = pi x (86/2)^2 -> A = 5808.8 mm^2 for 86 mm bore. 2 valves use 50% = 2904.4 mm^2; 3 valves 64% = 3717.6 mm^2 (+813.2 mm^2 vs 2v); 4 valves 68% = 3950.0 mm^2 (+232.4 vs 3v; +1045.6 vs 2v).
- DOHC: two direct-acting cams per head (intake cam + exhaust cam); 2, 4 or 5 valves (5 = three intake, two exhaust; third intake valve opens above a certain RPM automatically); 2v = 50%, 4v = 68%, 5v = 68% of bore area (same as 4v); 5v has less reciprocating mass than 4v, so excellent high RPM; VVL compatible (not on 5-valve DOHC); VVT on the intake cam only or both cams; most costly to engineer/produce; heavy/large heads; high CG in inline/V; standard in modern passenger cars; older DOHC used 2 valves.
- Multivalve benefits: better airflow, smaller lighter valves; cost more.

**Head Material** (Cast Iron, Aluminium, AlSi; no magnesium for heads): same character as block materials (iron: durable, muffles, heavy, higher CG; aluminium: lighter, lowers CG, better emissions, weaker, harder to work, needs sleeves note; AlSi: stronger than Al, porous, costlier than aluminium). Mixing head and block materials: "slight increase engineering time", "reduction in drop in reliability ratings" (text as written: causes a reliability reduction); but allows cost savings and CG improvement (cheap block, lighter head).

**Variable Valve Lift (VVL)** is described at the end of the family section (family-level choice: VVL available only on SOHC and DOHC). Pioneered by Honda VTEC, first used on 1989 Honda Integra XSi. Two sets of lobes (mild + aggressive), switch automatically at high RPM; costs complexity.

### 1B. VARIANT
Variant statistics: new variants of a family are "much cheaper, quicker, and easier to manufacture" than a new family. Quality slider appears on variant tabs (screenshot shows Quality 0 / +0; effect details NOT COVERED).

**Bottom End** (screenshot tabs: Crank | Conrods | Pistons | Variant Capacity | Quality). Affects smoothness, cost, reliability, weight, performance potential; "do not directly affect power and torque output".
- Crank options (screenshot): Cast Iron, Forged Steel, Billet Steel, Cast Iron Flat Plane, Forged Steel Flat Plane, Billet Steel Flat Plane. 
  - Cast iron: cheap, heavy, limits RPM, weakest. Forged steel: lighter, stronger, costlier/time-consuming. Billet steel: lightest, strongest, most labour-intensive, highest refinement.
  - Cross plane vs flat plane (only for 90deg V8): cross plane = journals at 90deg intervals ("+" shape); smoother with balancing shafts, heavier, limits high RPM, weaker exhaust scavenging at higher RPM; luxury V8s. Flat plane = journals 1&4 at 180deg to 2&3 ("|" shape); alternating banks improves exhaust scavenging; no balancing shafts needed so lighter; better high RPM; more vibration (less smooth); sports V8s.
- Conrods (screenshot/text): Cast; Heavy Duty Cast; Heavy Duty Forged; Lightweight Forged; Lightweight Titanium. Cast: cheap, weak. HD Cast: thicker, more torque-resistant, heavier, lowers RPM limit, smoother loss. HD Forged: lighter than HD cast, better RPM, expensive. Lightweight Forged: machined, even lighter, higher RPM ability, less torque resistance, more expensive. Lightweight Titanium: best torque and RPM resistance, protective coating (gold), smoothest, very high cost/production time. "Conrod failure is the most common cause of engine failure."
- Pistons: Cast; Heavy Duty Cast; Forged; Hypereutectic Cast; Low Friction Cast; Lightweight Forged. Cast: cheap, weak. HD Cast: thicker, heavier, lowers RPM limit. Forged: stronger, not lighter than cast, reduces pre-ignition (knock) and lowers octane requirement, less porous (more pollutant, louder), expensive. Hypereutectic: Al-Si alloy, best at muffling and absorbing pollution, about as good as cast for RPM/torque. Low Friction Cast: low-friction coating/shrunk skirts, best fuel efficiency, slightly lighter and better than cast, noisier, more fragile. Lightweight Forged: lightest, highest RPM limits, more pollution, noise, most expensive.
- Variant Capacity slider (bore / displacement / stroke for the variant): modify displacement within the family; "modifying the engine variant's capacity has no effect on the engine's outer dimensions, and little effect on its weight." Example: GM LS V8 family 4.8 L (293 CID) to 7.0 L (427 CID).

**Top End (Head)** (screenshot: Compression | Cam Profile | VVL Profile | VVT | Quality). Screenshot value examples: compression 9.6:1 (labels Low/Normal/High/Extreme), Cam Profile 25 (Low/Normal/Sport/Race labels), VVL Profile 40, VVT: None / VVT - Intake / VVT - All Cams.
- Compression ratio: ratio of cylinder volume at BDC to TDC (e.g. 9.6:1). Higher: more power and torque across range, better fuel efficiency; but higher pre-ignition (knock) risk, higher NOx. Counter by lower compression or higher octane fuel.
- Cam profile (lobe shape): aggressive cam = rougher idle ("sputter"). Mild cam: efficiency, low-RPM torque, smoothness, lower idle RPM, less pollution; but poor high RPM airflow, more valve float, more knock. Aggressive cam: high RPM breathing, improved cooling (allows higher compression), less valve float; but worse efficiency, poor low-RPM performance, rougher, idles at higher RPM, more pollution.
- VVL profile (second cam profile, only if family has VVL): used above a switch-over RPM determined automatically; always more aggressive than the regular profile; "Usually a difference of 15 to 25 between the regular cam and VVL cam" gives consistent torque; too large a gap = power loss mid-range; too small = no gain and not worth cost.
- VVT: cam phasing (changes when valves open/close, not duration; stepped adjustment: one timing at low RPM, another at high RPM); game auto-selects optimum timings and switchover; DOHC may use VVT on intake cam only to save cost. Options: None, VVT - Intake, VVT - All Cams.

**Forced Induction** (screenshot columns: Type | Setup | Intercooler | Presets | Compressor | Turbine | AR Ratio | Max. Boost)
- Type: Naturally Aspirated (atmospheric ~15 psi or 1 bar) or Turbo Charger. "The turbocharger option unlocks in 1975." (Only turbo; no supercharger mentioned.)
- Setup: "inline engines can only use a single turbo, meanwhile V and boxer engines can only use a twin turbo setup." Bearing types: Journal Bearing (cheapest, more friction, more lag) or Ball Bearing (less friction, better performance/lag, more cost; "By the mid-1980s ball bearing type turbos essentially become a straight upgrade").
- Intercooler: slider sized by power rating in hp (screenshots show e.g. 364 hp, 299 hp). Larger = better cooling, allows more boost; but more expensive, heavier, takes more engine bay space. Cooler air prevents pre-ignition and is denser. Based on PV = nRT.
- Presets: Fuel Economy, Performance, Race, Custom (set compressor and turbine diameter, AR ratio, max boost).
- Compressor (screenshot example 50.0 mm / 40.0 mm): size affects spool time and max boost; min/max sizes depend on engine size. Large: high-RPM performance, higher boost potential, but lag. Small: low-RPM, throttle response; limited max boost, high-RPM airflow restriction.
- Turbine (screenshot examples 50.0 mm / 40.0 mm): same trade-offs (large: high RPM/power but lag; small: fast spool but restrictive).
- AR Ratio (screenshot 0.800 / 0.805): inlet/outlet diameter ratio (compressor and turbine). <1.00 outlet smaller than inlet; 1.00 constant. Larger AR: better high RPM, higher potential, more lag. Smaller AR: faster spool, better low RPM, lower max.
- Max Boost (psi; screenshots 8.6 and 9.11 psi): target, not guaranteed. Example: 9.11 psi means up to 24.11 psi of air pressure in cylinders (adds to ~15 psi atmosphere). Higher boost: more power but more internal stress, knock risk, lag, more strain on fuel system (especially carburettors), reduced efficiency. Low boost keeps high compression and allows small turbo.
- Turbocharged engines muffle noise slightly; whine audible.

**Fuel System** (screenshot columns: Fuel System | Configuration | Intake | Fuel Type | Fuel Mixture | Ignition Timing | RPM Limit)
- Fuel Systems list: Carburetor, Injection (Mechanical Fuel Injection, Single Point EFI, Multi Point EFI, Direct Injection) per screenshot. Carburettors: five varieties; each has a maximum air-fuel ratio (AFR).
  - Single Barrel (Downdraft): simplest; poor performance; max AFR 13.2:1; available throughout the game; based on Holley single barrel; used until replaced by twin barrel in the 1960s.
  - Single Barrel Eco (Sidedraft): max AFR 14.2:1; leaner, saves fuel; low power potential; available throughout the game; based on SU.
  - Twin Barrel (Downdraft): max AFR 12.9:1; primary large + secondary small barrel; better airflow/efficiency; more complex; used from the 1960s to early 1990s; based on Hitachi.
  - Quad Barrel (Downdraft): max AFR 12.7:1; barrels open sequentially (25%, 50%, 75%, 100% throttle); most advanced downdraft; late 1960s to 1990s; high-end/performance cars; based on Holley 4-barrel.
  - DCOE (Sidedraft): max AFR 12.5:1; two large barrels; excellent flow; cost, poor fuel efficiency and emissions; cannot equip a standard intake (performance/race only); pre-FI high performance; based on Weber DCO-E.
  - Carburettors were the norm until the 1980s; fully replaced by FI by the 1990s.
  - Mechanical Fuel Injection (MFI): unlocks 1965; max AFR 12.5:1; one injector per cylinder port; single throttle body or throttle-per-cylinder; better response, smooth running; costly/complex; Bosch Mechanical Injection; slowly phased out in the 1980s.
  - Single Point EFI (SPEFI): unlocks 1978; max AFR 12.8:1; computer controlled single injector; reuses carburettor tooling/intakes; performance similar to 4-barrel carburettor; responds to engine conditions in real time; based on Bosch K-Jetronic.
  - Multi Point EFI (MPEFI): unlocks 1985; max AFR 12.5:1; injector per cylinder behind intake valves; precise control; more cost/complexity; Bosch KE-Jetronic; common late 1980s-2000s.
  - Direct Injection (GDI): unlocks 2001; max AFR 11.8:1 (richest); injectors in cylinder; best efficiency, response, minimal pollution; lightest (no cast iron intake manifold, plastic manifold); massive engineering/production cost; ~2000 psi (138 bar) fuel pressure, 12 V+ electronics; best for greatest power outputs.
- Configuration (throttle/carb arrangement): inline and boxer: one single throttle body or one throttle body per cylinder (screenshot values "Single" / "Per Cylinder"); V engines: one, two, or throttle-per-cylinder (SPEFI only one throttle body; mechanical injection cannot use two). Examples: Chrysler 440 CID V8 with three twin-barrel carbs; Nissan RB26-DETT throttle-per-cylinder MPEFI. More advanced configurations improve performance at higher cost and reduced fuel efficiency.
- Intake: Standard (well filtered and muffled: quiet, more reliable, less power); Performance (filtered but less muffling: more air, noisier, slight reliability penalty); Race (no filter/muffling: loudest, least reliable, lightest, most power). Intake also affects sounds. Intake type affects engine sound (together with cylinder layout, aspiration, muffler setup).
- Fuel Type (screenshot list): Regular Leaded - 92 RON; Super Leaded - 98 RON; Low Quality - 85 RON; Regular - 91 RON; Premium - 95 RON; Super - 98 RON; Ultimate - 100 RON. Text: octane rating = knock resistance (iso-octane 100, n-heptane 0); RON test at 600 RPM, MON at 900 RPM with preheating, MON typically 8-12 points below RON; AKI = (RON+MON)/2 used in US/Canada. Leaded fuel (tetraethyl lead) boosts octane but is toxic, and **catalytic converters cannot be used with leaded gas**; leaded fuel banned in nearly all the world mid-to-late 1970s (text). Year availability of each fuel grade in game: NOT COVERED. Higher octane fuel increases the vehicle's operating expense.
- Fuel Mixture slider (screenshot value 14.7; Rich/Lean ends): AFR in grams of air per 1 g fuel. Stoichiometric 14.7:1; >14.7 lean, <14.7 rich. Lean: efficiency, hotter, more knock, more NOx, less power. Rich: more power (to a point), cooler combustion, less pre-ignition; less efficient, more unburnt hydrocarbons; each fuel system has a maximum richness (see AFRs above); turbocharging allows richer than the limit (but backfires when tested). 
- Ignition Timing slider (screenshot value 50, Advanced/Retarded): default 50. >50 advanced: higher power at high RPM, efficiency gain, more pre-ignition risk. <50 retarded: better low RPM, less pre-ignition, lower efficiency.
- RPM Limit (screenshot value 7000, scale labels 12200 RPM / 7000 / 2000 RPM): formulas below. Higher limit raises power potential but needs strong internals, valvetrain (valve float), airflow; higher limit always less reliable on average.

**Exhaust System** (screenshot columns: Headers | Exhaust | Exhaust Diameter | Catalytic Converter | First Muffler | Second Muffler | Quality)
- Headers: Cast Log (cheapest, poor airflow); Short Cast (uses scavenging, slightly costlier); Tubular (welded stainless, lighter, better flow, more complex/noisier); Long Tubular (better airflow/scavenging, more complexity/noise); Race Tubular (hand built only, best flow, equal length pipes). "Note - Turbocharged engines can only use short cast headers."
- Exhaust: Single or Dual (inline: single only; V/boxer: single or dual; dual = better airflow, more cost and weight). Bypass valves: "No Valves" / "Bypass Valves" (closed = quiet normal driving, open = performance; increased cost/complexity; "may have more than two setting").
- Exhaust Diameter (screenshot 50.8 mm): wider = less restriction, more noise/cost/weight, lower efficiency; too wide loses scavenging; too narrow chokes and may cause pre-ignition. Example: 63.5 mm (2.5 in) is wide on a 1.6 L but narrow on a 6.0 L. Optimal depends on displacement.
- Catalytic Converter: None; Two-way (cheapest, poorest airflow; oxidises CO and hydrocarbons; used predominantly in the 1970s); Three-way (also reduces NOx; predominant from early 1980s); Three-way High Flow (current standard). None is the only option with leaded fuel; "selling a production car without a catalytic converter in the mid-1970s or later would be illegal" (real world). Reactions: 2CO + O2 -> 2CO2; hydrocarbon + O2 -> CO2 + H2O; 2CO + 2NO -> 2CO2 + N2; hydrocarbon + NO -> CO2 + H2O + N2; 2H2 + 2NO -> 2H2O + N2. Catalysts: palladium, iridium, platinum.
- Mufflers (First Muffler and Second Muffler; up to two): None (cheapest, lightest, loud, best flow); Baffled (cheapest/simplest, reflects noise, reduces airflow); Reverse Flow (most complex, quieter, superior muffling and airflow vs baffled); Straight-Through (simplest and best flowing, least quiet).

## 2. Size, weight, fit
- Engine physical size determined by: layout/cylinder count (inline long/narrow; V shorter/wider; boxer low/wide), bore (quadratic effect on block size and weight), stroke (slight effect), valvetrain/head (DOHC heads large and heavy; pushrod heads small, low CG), materials (iron heavy, magnesium lightest), intercooler (takes bay space), exhaust (dual adds weight), fuel system (GDI lightest), intake (race lightest).
- Variant capacity changes do not alter external dimensions; little effect on weight.
- Fit: "Cars in Automation come in a magnitude of different sizes ... engine bay sizes also depend on car to car." Engine size menu (Size / L / W / H / Fill %) with FWD/RWD/AWD/4x4 checkmarks and green fill bars shows how close to too large; the engine can fit but be incompatible with some drivetrain types due to size. Example: engine mounted longitudinally at front can access FWD, RWD, AWD, 4x4 in principle. Fitment affects drivetrain choices and weight balance. "How much area the engine takes up in the engine bay will affect engineering time and maintenance costs of the car. A fuller engine bay is harder to design, and harder to work on." Transverse vs longitudinal suitability: I5/60deg V narrow (transverse); I4/V6 60 / V8 60 transverse OK (Volvo, Taurus SHO); boxer cannot be transverse; 90deg Vs mostly longitudinal; I6 mostly longitudinal.
- Gearbox/drivetrain connection details: NOT COVERED on this page (only drivetrain type FWD/RWD/AWD/4x4 compatibility via size menu). Engine mounting types, placement (front/mid/rear) detail: NOT COVERED beyond examples (boxer rear/mid/front). "Use it in your car, so long as it fits."
- Page links to car trim and engine testing pages but contents are not reproduced here.

## 4. Summary / statistics screens
- Engine Statistics (screenshot example: "1998 cc DOHC 4V Inline 4", 142.3 hp @ 6100RPM, 175.6 lb-ft @ 2600RPM, 7000 RPM, 1420 $ etc.): lists max power (and RPM), max torque (and RPM), max RPM (redline), Performance index, Required cooling (affects car engineering), Smoothness, Responsiveness, Noise, Reliability, Yearly maintenance costs, Thermal efficiency (real limit ~60%; best production gas engines ~40%), Fuel octane requirement, Emissions, Weight, Engine volume/footprint, Material costs, Production time (months), Engineering time (months). Screenshot values (unlabeled icons): 72.5, 139 kg, 0.0L, 353.9, 33.7%, 105.2, 48.0, 90.9, 36.3, 135.2, 18.9, 50.5, 126.6. Mapping of icons to stats is not given.
- Variant Statistics: viewable on any tab after variant completed; hover shows what each number is; live comparison old (right) vs new (left) with green=better, red=worse; example noise 20.5 -> 18.9, weight 137.4 -> 138.7 kg; can hold ("save") current tune, compare against held tune; buttons: store, delete ("not-allowed" symbol), revert (undo).
- Engine Testing: dynamometer; three dials (torque/power; air pressure in in. Hg for vacuum or psi boost; RPM); NA tops at 0 in. Hg and 0 psi; two buttons: auto run idle to redline, manual rev. Sound depends on four things: cylinder layout, aspiration method, intake type, muffler setup.

## 5. Formulas and numbers (exact)
- Displacement = pi x (bore / 2)^2 x stroke x cylinder count.
- Bore area A = pi x (86 / 2)^2 = 5808.8 mm^2; valve area use: 2v 50% = 2904.4 mm^2; 3v 64% = 3717.6 mm^2; 4v 68% = 3950.0 mm^2; 5v 68%.
- Bore/stroke limits: 50 mm to 120 mm. Default 86 mm x 86 mm.
- Average Piston Speed = 2 x stroke x (RPM / 60) (mm gives mm/s); RPM = [Average Piston Speed / (2 x stroke)] x 60.
- hp = (tq x RPM) / 5252 (lb.ft); kW = (tq x RPM) / 9.5488 (Nm).
- Combustion: 25 O2 + 2 C8H18 -> 16 CO2 + 18 H2O. Stoichiometric AFR 14.7:1.
- Gas law PV = nRT (intercooling). AKI = (RON + MON)/2. Boost example 9.11 psi -> 24.11 psi absolute (atmosphere about 15 psi).
- Ignition timing default 50 (>50 advanced, <50 retarded). VVL profile gap recommended about 15 to 25 over regular cam.
- Max AFR per fuel system: Single barrel 13.2:1; Eco sidedraft 14.2:1; Twin barrel 12.9:1; Quad barrel 12.7:1; DCOE 12.5:1; MFI 12.5:1; SPEFI 12.8:1; MPEFI 12.5:1; GDI 11.8:1.
- Unlock years: Inline-5 1970; V6 60deg and 90deg 1970; Turbo 1975; MFI 1965; SPEFI 1978; MPEFI 1985; GDI 2001; ball bearing turbos "by the mid-1980s" an upgrade.
- GDI pressure 2000 psi (138 bar). Revs: screenshot RPM limit scale 2000 to 12200 RPM, selected 7000.
- Octane scale: iso-octane 100, n-heptane 0, RON test 600 RPM, MON test 900 RPM, MON 8 to 12 below RON; Fuel types listed in UI: 92/98 leaded, 85, 91, 95, 98, 100 RON.
- Thermal efficiency: theoretical limit about 60%, best production about 40%.
